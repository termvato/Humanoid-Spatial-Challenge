# Fork SceneDiff, then own the unknowns

As of October 2026 the strongest method for this exact task is **SceneDiff**. It is training-free, released under MIT with code and data, and takes two unposed videos. It runs a joint Pi3 reconstruction and SAM regions, compares depth and DINOv3 features across co-visible frames, and greedily matches objects across the two videos to label each one Added, Removed or Moved. Its best number is still only **22.8 object-per-scene AP on its own real benchmark**, so the problem is wide open, and the paper's own margins shrink to a near tie on a competitor's dataset. Every alternative family is weaker for a 3-4 day phone-video build. Gaussian-splat methods need dense captures and over 30 minutes per scene and score near zero on free trajectories. Pure VLM pipelines are poor at spatial change. Newer trained or online methods (VSCD, O-SCD, GS-DIFF) have no confirmed code. The best build is to fork SceneDiff as a working baseline on day one. Then add what neither it nor any benchmark provides: true 3D outputs, a region-level **changed / unchanged / unobserved** map built from free-space evidence, change descriptions written from the 3D facts, and a costmap-style output a navigation stack can consume. Deployed robots still mostly handle change by re-recording maps or having operators edit them, so a trustworthy, uncertainty-aware change list is a real product gap that Humanoid's own job postings point at. Measured against the evidence, three of the user's plans need revising. Isaac Sim 5.1 is officially below spec on the 8 GB laptop. The SceneDiff dataset makes heavy synthetic generation much less necessary. And the "keyless" default still needs gated Hugging Face checkpoints.

## SceneDiff leads, but every method wins on its home benchmark

SceneDiff (Wu, Lin, Che, Tiwari, Zou, Wang, Hoiem; UIUC) matches the challenge brief almost exactly. It takes two captures of the same scene along different trajectories and identifies added, removed and moved objects while ignoring viewpoint and occlusion differences ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16908)). The pipeline runs **Pi3 jointly on both videos** for depth, pose and intrinsics, normalised to a unit cube. It then pairs frames by bidirectional co-visibility (over 50%, or the single best match). Next it scores SAM regions with depth reprojection, DINOv3 feature reprojection and region-level DINOv3 matching, and thresholds those scores with maximum-entropy (Kapur) thresholding. Finally it merges regions into instances ConceptGraphs-style and matches instances one-to-one across videos at cosine similarity above 0.7 to assign the change type ([arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16908v2)). The coordinator verified that the code is public at github.com/yuqunw/scene_diff under MIT, last pushed 2026-08-29, and that the benchmark is an ungated MIT single zip on Hugging Face ([GitHub](https://github.com/yuqunw/scene_diff); [HF dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/yuqun/SceneDiff)). The "ECCV 2026" label appears only in the README. The arXiv page lists no venue.

The benchmark is the most useful asset in the field for this project: **350 real video pairs, 50 scenes, 1,009 changed objects**, with per-frame masks, object names and change types. It has two subsets. SD-V has 200 pairs captured by 7 people, including some outdoor scenes. SD-K has 150 egocentric kitchen pairs derived from HD-Epic. The splits are 50 validation pairs per subset and 250 test pairs, kept scene-disjoint, and there is no training split ([arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16908v2); [HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/yuqun/SceneDiff)). SD-K inherits HD-Epic's upstream terms, so the MIT tag on the card may not cover it cleanly.

The headline numbers need scepticism on three counts. First, they drift between revisions. Version 1 reported SD-V per-view AP 49.6, RC-3D AP50 68.7 and ChangeSim F1 57.6. Version 2 reports object-per-image AP 43.8, RC-3D 65.3 and ChangeSim 57.0, and the relative-gain claims fell from figures a summary site quotes as 94% and 37.4% to **53.0% and 30.6%** ([v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16908v1); [v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16908v2); [papers.cool](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2512.16908)). Second, baseline scores on external benchmarks are copied from other papers rather than re-run. Third, the margin depends on whose data is used. On SceneDiff's own SD-V, 3DGS-CD scores 0.5 object-per-scene AP, yet 3DGS-CD reports F1 of 96.4-98.8 on its own dataset ([SceneDiff v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16908v2); [3DGS-CD](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.03706)). On MV3DCD's PASLCD benchmark, SceneDiff's 47.3 mIoU against MV3DCD's 46.1 is effectively a tie. Each method wins at home, and no third-party re-evaluation of SceneDiff exists. A submission that runs SceneDiff on its own phone captures and reports where it fails is producing evidence nobody else has.

SceneDiff's admitted failures mark out the space a submission can work in. They are identical objects swapping places; pose failure in low light, on textureless surfaces or with low overlap (2 of 200 SD-V pairs); large cluttered scenes with repetitive items (11 of 200); strong lighting changes that flag static pillows; and people in frame, who must be masked manually. Runtime is about **159 s per pair on one A40**, and SAM mask generation dominates at about 4 s per image ([arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16908v2)). In an ablation, VGGT "inverts the scene dynamics" when most movable objects change together: because it regresses in the first frame's coordinates, it marks walls as moved. That is a concrete reason to prefer a reference-free model such as Pi3 ([arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16908v2)).

The rest of the field is either further from the brief or unverifiable. **3DGS-CD** gives 3D masks plus rigid transforms for moved objects. However, it needs more than 150 pre-change images covering the moved objects, runs on a 2023-era stack (Python 3.8, nerfstudio, tiny-cuda-nn) and was last pushed in February 2025 ([GitHub](https://github.com/520xyxyzq/3DGS-CD)). **MV3DCD** (CVPR 2025) optimises a 3DGS per scene, outputs only binary 2D masks and has no recognised licence ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.03911v2); [GitHub API](https://api.github.com/repos/Chumsy0725/MV3DCD)). **VSCD** (ICML 2026) is framed for exactly two unaligned indoor videos. It is trained on over 1.1M mostly simulated frames, but it outputs only per-frame pixel masks with no change type, and the abstract gives neither numbers nor code ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20821)). O-SCD (CVPR 2026, online at more than 10 FPS), GS-DIFF (Gaussian-primitive comparison with a per-primitive observability term), SCaR-3D and the DSG scene-graph method publish no confirmed code and no comparison against SceneDiff ([O-SCD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.12370); [GS-DIFF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07203); [SCaR-3D](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.22830); [DSG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00619)). The VLM baseline in SceneDiff's own table (Qwen2.5-VL with SAM 3) reaches only **5.3 object-per-scene AP** on SD-V ([arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16908v2)).

## Ranked technique choices for a four-day build

The overall strategy ranking is clear. First and best, fork SceneDiff, run it on SD-V validation and on the user's own pair on day one, then extend it. Second, build a lean ConceptGraphs/OASIS-Map-style per-session object map and match objects across sessions. This is about 500 lines and fully under the user's control, but it gives up a working baseline. Third and fourth, both rejected: 3DGS change detection (too slow, dense capture, near-zero AP on free trajectories) and VLM-first detection (5.3 AP, and weak spatial reasoning, covered below). The table ranks choices per stage within the recommended strategy.

| Stage | 1st choice | 2nd choice | Avoid or demote | Key pro / con |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Poses and depth (plain video) | Pi3X, joint pass over keyframes of both videos | DA3 (Giant/Large for research; Apache Base/Small/Metric) or DA3-Streaming | VGGT-1B as default | Pi3 and DA3 hold up in independent tests where VGGT does not; Pi3/Pi3X and DA3-Giant weights are non-commercial |
| Poses and depth (sensor path) | ARKit poses + LiDAR depth from Stray Scanner, confidence 2 only | MapAnything conditioned on ARKit poses and intrinsics (Apache weights exist) | Monocular metric scale alone | Sensor path is metric and gravity-aligned; monocular scale was about 17% off in one test |
| Cross-session alignment | Joint feed-forward pass (SceneDiff style) | Separate ARKit sessions, gravity-constrained 4-DoF RANSAC, then robust ICP on floor, walls and large furniture | Learned registration trained on static scans | Joint pass is proven on this task; it is non-metric unless rescaled |
| Keyframes | 40-60 per video, sharpest frame per time bin | Chunks of 100-400 frames, Sim(3)-aligned | Full-rate video | Feed-forward models cannot take full trajectories |
| Object masks | SAM 3 / 3.1 video predictor | SAM 2 + DINOv2 (Apache) | Per-frame automatic SAM on every frame | SAM is the runtime bottleneck at about 4 s per image; SAM 3 checkpoints are gated |
| Change evidence | SceneDiff depth + feature reprojection, plus explicit free-space voting into 3 states | OASIS-Map 50%-observed rule at object level | Treating "missing" as "removed" | Only free-space evidence separates removed from unobserved |
| Object matching | Dense DINOv3 patch correspondences + Hungarian (OASIS-Map) | SceneDiff greedy cosine above 0.7 | Geometry-dominated matching cost | Moved objects change position by definition; add a rigid-fit check after matching |
| Ambiguous pairs | VLM yes/no/unsure on two separate crops | Relational cues from neighbours (REMIND) | Side-by-side composite images | Composites hurt VLM accuracy in 9 of 10 categories |
| Labels | Open-vocab masks + VLM relabel of best crops (Qwen3-VL, Apache) | CLIP/SigLIP retrieval | Trusting raw 3D open-vocab labels | Open-vocab 3D APs are single digits on hard sets |
| Language | Templates from structured 3D diff JSON; optional Claude rewrite constrained to the JSON | Qwen3-VL rewrite | VLM describing change from raw frames | Best VLM gets 59.9% on spatial change vs 95% for humans |
| Robot output | Object change list + 2D costmap delta with "unknown" as elevated cost | Scene-graph diff for an LLM planner | Binary masks only | Matches how Nav2 and nvblox consume change |

The reconstruction ranking rests on the only independent evidence available. On an asset-inspection benchmark run by non-developers, pose AUC30 in an office scene was **DA3 0.549-0.560, Pi3 0.537-0.538 and VGGT 0.121-0.137**, against 0.901-0.951 for COLMAP. That benchmark also needed batch limits of 400, 200 and 100 images per pass respectively ([arXiv 2603.17358](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.17358)). The same authors found feed-forward models do best when each view sees much of the scene, which favours a room walk-through. DA3's self-reported margin over VGGT changes between revisions: 35.7% / 23.6% in one paper version, 44.3% / 25.1% on the model card ([DA3 arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.10647); [DA3-BASE card](https://huggingface.co/depth-anything/DA3-BASE)). Licences matter here because the best-performing weights are non-commercial: Pi3 and Pi3X weights are CC BY-NC ([Pi3 GitHub](https://github.com/yyfz/Pi3)), DA3-Giant and Large are CC BY-NC while Base, Small and Metric-Large are Apache ([DA3 GitHub](https://github.com/ByteDance-Seed/Depth-Anything-3)), and MapAnything ships an Apache checkpoint alongside a stronger CC BY-NC one ([MapAnything GitHub](https://github.com/facebookresearch/map-anything)). For an internship submission, non-commercial research use is acceptable as long as the README says so.

For alignment, the papers that work on this task all align against the static majority of the scene. SceneDiff uses one joint Pi3 pass. A practitioner project, room-diff, runs one COLMAP SfM over both videos so that moved objects fall out as RANSAC outliers ([room-diff](https://github.com/viv344/room-diff)). 3DGS-CD localises post-change images against the pre-change SfM map ([3DGS-CD](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.03706)). The RIO/3RScan authors warn that hand-crafted and 3DMatch-style descriptors are "very susceptible to geometry changes" ([RIO paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_ICCV_2019/papers/Wald_RIO_3D_Object_Instance_Re-Localization_in_Changing_Indoor_Environments_ICCV_2019_paper.pdf)). With Stray Scanner, each session already has metric, gravity-aligned ARKit poses and 192x256 LiDAR depth with confidence maps ([Stray data model](https://excellent-legal-071.notion.site/Stray-Scanner-Data-Model-aea95b702b484d41b2de12d2e90781ce)). Gravity reduces cross-session alignment to yaw plus translation. That claim rests on background knowledge of ARKit, not on a fetched source, and the camera-axis convention (OpenGL-style for ARKit vs OpenCV for Pi3 and MapAnything) must be checked on real files before any numbers are trusted.

For matching, OASIS-Map (Oxford DRS) is the most relevant recent design. It aggregates forward/backward DINOv3 patch matches per mask pair, runs Hungarian assignment on the proportion of matching patches, and uses geometry only afterwards, through centroid displacement, to separate static from moved ([OASIS-Map](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14899)). Its own 3RScan numbers show how hard the problem remains: change-detection F1 is **0.353 for moved and 0.241 for appear/disappear**. On removed-object association, plain ConceptGraphs run per session beats it (0.952 vs 0.842) ([OASIS-Map](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14899)). The VLM evidence points the same way. On VLM-SubtleBench the best model, GPT-5-thinking, scores **59.9% on spatial change against 95% for humans**, existence accuracy falls below 60% once a scene holds more than 32 objects, and placing the two images side by side in one composite input hurt accuracy in 9 of 10 categories ([VLM-SubtleBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.07888v1)). That benchmark tested Claude Sonnet 4, not current Claude models. The design consequence is that geometry decides the change type, and the language model only names objects, checks one ambiguous pair at a time, and phrases facts it is handed.

A day plan follows from the table. Day 1: capture two Stray pairs with planned moves, adds and removes; get SceneDiff running on SD-V validation and on one own pair; write the Stray loader. Day 2: lift SceneDiff instances to 3D boxes; build the 3-state visibility voxel map; score Pi3X and DA3 poses against ARKit using Sim(3) Umeyama alignment, ATE, and AbsRel against LiDAR. Day 3: train head A, the change verifier (detailed below), and add matching with a rigid-fit check and template language. Day 4: costmap delta, visualisation, evaluation tables and README.

## OASIS-Map claims "unknown" for objects, but nobody outputs it for regions

The notes disagree on whether any method outputs an explicit unobserved state, and both are partly right. **OASIS-Map does output "unknown", but only at object level and only for unmatched objects.** An unmatched object is called appeared or disappeared only if at least 50% of its 3D volume lies inside the region the other session observed; otherwise it is labelled unknown ([OASIS-Map](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14899)). That is a real precedent, and a submission must not claim to be first to reason about it. What OASIS-Map does not do is produce a region-level map in which every part of the shared scene is marked changed, unchanged or unobserved. Its unknown label is not evaluated as an output either: its reported metrics cover static, moved and appear/disappear. OASIS-Map is also an RGB-D mapping method evaluated on 3RScan, not a phone-video change detector.

Among the change-detection methods and benchmarks proper, **none outputs a region-level three-state result**. SceneDiff handles visibility implicitly. It pairs frames by co-visibility, keeps only depth differences where the before-surface sits in front of the after-surface, treats the opposite sign as possible occlusion, and excludes regions more than 60% masked by visibility ([SceneDiff v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16908v1)). Everything it does not flag is simply "no change". GS-DIFF uses a per-primitive observability term internally but outputs change maps ([GS-DIFF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07203)). 3DGS-CD only fuses views in which at least 80% of an object's projected points are visible ([3DGS-CD](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.03706)). No benchmark, whether SceneDiff, PASLCD, RC-3D or ChangeSim, scores an unobserved class.

The closest working implementation is **room-diff**, a non-peer-reviewed practitioner project. For each point it takes a per-frame vote: consistent, free (the frame sees through the point, which is the only evidence of change), occluded, or unobserved. A point counts as changed only with at least 3 free votes, a free ratio of at least 70%, and no nearby point in the other cloud ([room-diff](https://github.com/viv344/room-diff)). In robotics mapping, Khronos labels points as occluded, consistent or evidence of absence by keeping rays from background vertices to observing poses ([Khronos](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.13817v1)). The underlying principle goes back at least to Underwood et al., who criticised methods that "implicitly assume that unmeasured space is unoccupied" ([ICRA 2013](https://www.acfr.usyd.edu.au/papers/pdfs/Underwood-ICRA13-ChangeDetection.pdf)).

So the defensible originality claim is narrow but real. A submission can be the first, within the phone-video change-detection literature, to make **unobserved a first-class, region-level, evaluated output**. That would mean a voxel map with three states from free-space evidence, a visualisation of it, and a metric for it: for example, precision on "changed" restricted to co-observed space, plus the share of ground-truth changes that fall in unobserved space and are correctly withheld. The voxel recipe is to voxelise each session at 2-5 cm. A surface voxel from session A is unchanged if B saw it occupied, removed if B saw free space through it with enough votes, and unknown otherwise, and the rule runs symmetrically for added. False "free" votes come mostly from LiDAR edge pixels and low-confidence depth, so those must be masked.

Four further gaps are open and cheap to exploit. **3D outputs**: SceneDiff evaluates and outputs 2D masks although it builds 3D instances internally. Exporting oriented 3D boxes and a motion vector for each moved object meets the brief's "in 3D" requirement directly. **Learned calibration**: SceneDiff uses hand-set Kapur thresholds, so head A can be a small verifier on frozen DINO features plus geometric residuals (depth disagreement, free-vote ratio, visibility fraction) that replaces those thresholds. Geometric cues are the inputs most likely to transfer from synthetic to real data ([arXiv 2407.12449](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.12449)). **Identical-object swaps**: a rigid-fit check on matched pairs, plus neighbour-relation cues of the kind used for re-identification in REMIND, targets SceneDiff's admitted worst failure ([REMIND](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.09267)). **Grounded language with a fidelity check**: no evaluation of JSON-to-prose faithfulness for change captions was found. Checking that every sentence maps back to a fact in the 3D diff is both easy and novel. A sixth, smaller contribution is to report how detection degrades under injected pose noise, using ARKit as the reference, since pose error looks like change.

## Deployed robots re-record maps instead of patching them

Industry practice is far behind research. Boston Dynamics' GraphNav localises against data stored at recording time. Its docs list "the environment has changed dramatically" as a cause of poor localisation, the robot refuses to navigate when lost, and the recommended fix for site changes is to **record a new map** ([BD Localization](https://dev.bostondynamics.com/docs/concepts/autonomy/localization); [BD GraphNav Service](https://dev.bostondynamics.com/docs/concepts/autonomy/graphnav_service)). Directed Exploration handles blocked paths, not stale maps ([BD Directed Exploration](https://dev.bostondynamics.com/docs/concepts/autonomy/directed_exploration)). ANYbotics handles change through human-in-the-loop editing of "Environment Objects" ([ANYbotics 23.02](https://www.anybotics.com/news/23-02-product-release-anybotics)). Commercial "change detection" exists, but as inspection: Spot's Orbit runs vision-language prompts on photos of points of interest and flags spills or missing extinguishers, with no published accuracy figures ([BD blog](https://bostondynamics.com/blog/see-your-facility/)).

Open-source navigation handles change only at costmap level. NVIDIA's nvblox assumes a static scene by default and sends people into a separate occupancy layer that decays over time, following the rule that people belong in the costmap but not in the reconstruction ([nvblox arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.00626); [NVIDIA blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/build-high-performance-robotic-applications-with-nvidia-isaac-ros-developer-preview-3/)). Nav2 has obstacle and voxel layers with raytrace clearing plus mask-driven keepout and speed filters, and a long-standing complaint that moved obstacles leave **stale "trails" that block the robot** ([Nav2 KeepoutFilter](https://api.nav2.org/nav2-jazzy/html/classnav2__costmap__2d_1_1KeepoutFilter.html); [ROS Answers](https://answers.ros.org/question/403262/obstacle-and-voxel-layer-not-raytracing-free-space-properly/)). Research systems sit in between. Khronos separates short-term dynamics from long-term change and gives each object appearance and disappearance times ([Khronos](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.13817)). POCD keeps a per-object stationarity score updated by Bayesian fusion ([POCD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.01202v2)). 3D Variable Scene Graphs predict which objects are likely to change and report 66% faster active change detection ([3D VSG](https://arxiv.org/html/2209.07896v2)). There is no standard interface between an object-level change model and a costmap.

Humanoid has published nothing technical on mapping, but its stated direction is unambiguous. A London posting for a spatial-intelligence autonomy engineer asks for "long-term persistent spatial memory", 3D representations that "track and interpret environment state", and foundation geometry models in the navigation stack ([posting mirror](https://www.vcbacked.co/jobs/role/humanoid-senior-autonomy-engineer-spatial-intelligence-67f1d4a4)). In company materials, the KinetIQ System 2 layer calls tools including "perception queries" and "re-localization" and consumes status reports from navigation and perception. The same materials concede that current VLMs "perceive dynamics and physics poorly" ([thehumanoid.ai/ai](https://thehumanoid.ai/ai/)). Its reported deployments, tote handling at Siemens and box handling for Schaeffler, are dominated by semi-static change: totes, pallets and carts moving over hours ([Robotics 24/7](https://www.robotics247.com/article/humanoid-siemens-complete-proof-of-concept-to-test-humanoid-robots-in-industrial-logistics); [AI Business](https://aibusiness.com/robotics/humanoid-schaeffler-bring-thousands-robots-to-factories)). All of these are company claims.

A submission therefore reads as credible to this team if its output works at three levels. The first is an object change list (id; type added, removed, moved or unknown; label; 3D box; old and new pose; evidence count; confidence) for the spatial-memory layer. The second is a 2D costmap delta for locomotion. The third is a language-addressable query interface of the kind System 2 calls. One design rule follows from Nav2's stale-trail problem and from safety. A false "removed" clears a real obstacle, which is a safety failure, while a false "added" only costs efficiency. So clearing should need more evidence than marking, and unobserved space should carry raised but not lethal cost. That rule is an inference, not a published industry standard, and should be presented as a design choice.

| D4 user decides; 6-10 own pairs | Keep, but add SceneDiff validation pairs as real training and tuning data | 100 real validation pairs, MIT, scene-disjoint from test |
| D5 heads A and B, C only if time | Keep A, simplify B, keep C deferred | No learned cross-session matcher with code exists to build on; OASIS-style matching plus a small learned rescorer is enough |
| D7 3080 deferred | Keep deferred; it does not fix Isaac's VRAM floor | Isaac Sim 5.1 minimum is 16 GB |
| I1 Isaac procedural pairs | Argue against as the main data source | Below spec on 8 GB; Replicator quirks; real labelled data now exists |
| I2 sim+real co-training sweep | Demote to an optional ablation | Evidence is from manipulation policies; best ratio swung from 50% (bad) to 99% (best) to 99.9% (bad) |

**D1** holds up, with two caveats. Plain video is the harder, recognised problem, and joint feed-forward reconstruction is exactly what the current state of the art uses. But the decision names VGGT as an example, and the independent pose numbers plus SceneDiff's own ablation argue against VGGT as the default ([arXiv 2603.17358](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.17358); [SceneDiff v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16908v2)). Pi3X, run jointly over both sessions, is the stronger default, with MapAnything as the pose-conditioned sensor path. The second caveat is that the three-state map depends on depth quality more than any other component. Learned depth with roughly 17% scale error ([room-diff](https://github.com/viv344/room-diff)) produces false free-space votes, so the demo should show both paths side by side rather than leaving LiDAR as a footnote.

**D3** has a hidden contradiction. A reviewer cannot run SAM 3 without requesting gated access on Hugging Face ([SAM 3 GitHub](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam3)), and DINOv3 weights also require sign-up and approval ([DINOv3 licence](https://ai.meta.com/resources/models-and-libraries/dinov3-license)). SceneDiff's README itself expects a gated DINOv3 checkpoint ([GitHub](https://github.com/yuqunw/scene_diff)). The fix is a default profile built on SAM 2 and Apache-licensed DINOv2, which SceneDiff already ablates, with SAM 3 and DINOv3 behind a flag. The accuracy cost of the swap was not extracted from the paper and should be measured. Keeping a VLM out of the detection loop is strongly supported by the SubtleBench evidence, so the template-first language design stands.

**I1 is where the evidence pushes back hardest.** Isaac Sim 5.1's official minimum is **16 GB of VRAM**, so the 8 GB RTX 4070 laptop is below spec, and the 4.5 minimum of 8 GB was raised for 5.x ([Isaac Sim 5.1 requirements](https://docs.isaacsim.omniverse.nvidia.com/5.1.0/installation/requirements.html); [4.5 requirements](https://docs.isaacsim.omniverse.nvidia.com/4.5.0/installation/requirements.html)). The same page says 5.1.0 is no longer supported, and forum reports describe Replicator writer loops running at about 8 FPS and noisy segmentation ([NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/fps-drop-in-isaac-sim-depending-on-camera-creation-method/326207)). The RTX 3080 also has less than 16 GB in every variant, so D7's render-worker plan does not remove the risk; moving rendering to the cloud would need an RT-core GPU such as an L4 or L40S, not an A100. More importantly, the reason for synthetic pairs was a shortage of labelled real change data, and SceneDiff's 100 real validation pairs largely remove that shortage. If simulation is wanted, AI2-THOR's rearrangement tooling is a far cheaper source of exact change ground truth. It changes 1 to 5 objects per episode, and multiple sources state its assets are Apache-licensed ([ai2thor-rearrangement](https://github.com/allenai/ai2thor-rearrangement)). The best use of Isaac Sim within 3-4 days is the robot-navigation demo, consuming the costmap delta in one scene, rather than a dataset engine.

**I2 and D5** follow from that. The co-training evidence comes from manipulation policies, where the best sim share on one task was 99% while 50% and 99.9% did markedly worse ([arXiv 2503.24361](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.24361v2)). The only directly perception-relevant results are that frozen DINO features and geometric inputs transfer better than raw appearance ([DINOv3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.10104); [arXiv 2407.12449](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.12449)). Curriculum learning shows mostly marginal benefits outside noisy-label settings ([When Do Curricula Work](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03107v2)). Head A should therefore be trained on real candidates from SceneDiff validation plus the user's own pairs, split by scene, and must beat SceneDiff's own thresholding on the SceneDiff test split and on the user's held-out room. Without that comparison the learned component cannot be shown to add anything. Head B can be a small rescorer over OASIS-style match statistics plus rigid-fit residuals rather than a full learned matcher. A sim-only versus real-only versus mixed comparison is worth running only if synthetic pairs come cheaply.

## Conclusion

The field has quietly converged on a recipe: reconstruct jointly, compare frozen foundation features across co-visible views, and match objects after the fact. Absolute accuracy, however, has not converged: SceneDiff scores 22.8 AP on its own data, and OASIS-Map's F1 on moved objects is 0.35. That combination favours a submission whose originality lies in honesty about what the cameras did not see, rather than in a new detector. The underexploited asset is the asymmetry between evidence of absence and absence of evidence. It separates removed from unobserved, it is what Nav2's stale-trail problem lacks, and no change-detection benchmark currently scores it.

For this build, the evidence moves effort away from synthetic data generation, which open real labels have largely made unnecessary, and towards a forked baseline, a three-state 3D map, and evaluation that is reported openly: self-reported numbers re-run on new captures, against ARKit and LiDAR references. A reviewer at a humanoid company running semi-static factory cells will value a system that says "I am not sure, rescan here" as much as one that says "the chair moved 40 cm".

## Update 2026-10-12: recent papers check

- **GS-Pool** (Keren-Gil, Gain, Marais; arXiv 2610.06688, 5 Oct 2026). Object-level change detection on two per-visit 3DGS models: DINOv3 features distilled onto Gaussians, SAM2 masks lifted into an object pool, four per-primitive change scores ranked against a null of unchanged objects. Needs COLMAP poses and per-visit splats (minutes per member on a 4090, excluding reconstruction). Self-reported PASLCD mIoU/F1 0.751/0.846 vs GS-Diff 0.644/0.758. No added/removed/moved typing (future work), no unobserved output (only "covered" primitives, inside 2+ frusta of the other visit, are decided), no code repo, not evaluated on SceneDiff. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06688))
- **No new paper found that reports numbers on the SceneDiff benchmark**, and still none that outputs a region-level unobserved class.
- **VGGT-Omega** (CVPR 2026). Feed-forward reconstruction, reported more robust than Pi3X and VGGT on wide viewpoint spans by an independent stress test ([arXiv 2609.24839](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.24839)). Weights gated with automated approval (some rejections reported), licence reportedly CC BY-NC 4.0, and the repo itself warns its 1B benchmark numbers may be inflated by contamination (notice 18 Aug 2026). ([GitHub](https://github.com/facebookresearch/vggt-omega)) Candidate for S5 if access is granted.
- **OASIS-Map** (arXiv 2607.14899) already covered above; it reports no free-space or unobserved reasoning.
